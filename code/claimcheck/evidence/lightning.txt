The saying that lightning never strikes the same place twice is false. Lightning often strikes the same place repeatedly, especially tall, isolated or pointed objects. The Empire State Building in New York City is struck roughly 20 to 25 times in an average year. Lightning rods work by giving a strike a safe, low-resistance path to the ground. A lightning bolt can heat the air around it to about 30,000 degrees Celsius, roughly five times hotter than the surface of the Sun.
